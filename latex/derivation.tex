\begin{abstract}
This document derives a novel, recursive, online algorithm for estimating the asymptotic variance of a reward function defined on a Continuous-Time Markov Chain (CTMC) using single-trajectory samples. Our approach represents the continuous-time analog of the linear stochastic approximation (SA) method proposed by Agrawal, Prashanth, and Maguluri (2024) for Discrete-Time Markov Chains (DTMCs). We utilize the continuous-time Poisson equation to construct a temporal difference (TD)-like learning scheme that requires $O(1)$ computation and memory per step. By scaling the stochastic approximation updates with observed holding times, we show that the jump-chain discrete-time updates converge exactly to continuous-time stationary expectations. We provide a rigorous analysis of the value function estimator's stability and present extensions under linear function approximation (LFA).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Physical Intuition}

In stochastic processes, moving from discrete-time Markov chains (DTMCs) to continuous-time Markov chains (CTMCs) represents a transition from discrete steps to a continuous flow. This transition simplifies the mathematical structure of long-term fluctuations.

To build physical intuition, consider a traveler wandering through a house with several rooms. Each room $x \in S$ has a specific room temperature $f(x)$. 
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Discrete-Time (DTMC):} The traveler teleports to a new room once every minute (discrete steps).
    \item \textbf{Continuous-Time (CTMC):} The traveler stays in each room $x$ for a continuous, exponentially distributed duration of time with rate $q(x)$ before moving to another room.
\end{itemize}

Over a very long journey, the traveler spends a fraction of their time in room $x$ given by the stationary probability $\pi_x$. The long-run average temperature they experience is the \textbf{stationary mean}:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{f} = \mathbb{E}_\pi[f(X)] = \sum_{x \in S} \pi_x f(x)
\end{equation}

\section{The Value Function and the Poisson Equation}

To understand the fluctuations around the stationary mean, we define the \textbf{value function} $V(x)$ (also called the expected head-start or deviation function). This function answers the question: \textit{``If the traveler starts in room $x$ today, what is the net cumulative temperature difference they will experience over their entire future journey compared to someone who starts in the long-run average condition?''}

Formally, the value function $V: S \to \mathbb{R}$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    V(x) = \int_0^\infty \mathbb{E}[ f(X_t) - \bar{f} \mid X_0 = x ] \, dt
\end{equation}

In a CTMC, the transition rates are governed by the infinitesimal generator matrix $Q$, where $Q_{xy}$ is the transition rate from state $x$ to state $y$ for $x \neq y$, and $Q_{xx} = -q(x) = -\sum_{y \neq x} Q_{xy}$. By the properties of the generator, the expected rate of change of our future head-start must perfectly balance the instantaneous temperature deviation:
\begin{equation}
    Q V(x) = -(f(x) - \bar{f})
\end{equation}
In vector-matrix form, this is the \textbf{continuous-time Poisson equation}:
\begin{equation}
    -Q \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{f} - \bar{f}\mathbf{1}
\end{equation}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the Poisson Equation]
By definition of the generator matrix $Q$ acting on the function $V$:
\begin{equation}
    Q V(x) = \lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{\mathbb{E}[V(X_h) \mid X_0 = x] - V(x)}{h}
\end{equation}
Substituting the definition of $V(x)$:
\begin{align}
    \mathbb{E}[V(X_h) \mid X_0 = x] &= \int_0^\infty \mathbb{E}[ f(X_{t+h}) - \bar{f} \mid X_0 = x ] \, dt \nonumber \\
    &= \int_h^\infty \mathbb{E}[ f(X_s) - \bar{f} \mid X_0 = x ] \, ds
\end{align}
Subtracting $V(x)$ from both sides:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}[V(X_h) \mid X_0 = x] - V(x) = -\int_0^h \mathbb{E}[ f(X_s) - \bar{f} \mid X_0 = x ] \, ds
\end{equation}
Dividing by $h$ and taking the limit as $h \to 0^+$:
\begin{align}
    Q V(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{-1}{h} \int_0^h \mathbb{E}[ f(X_s) - \bar{f} \mid X_0 = x ] \, ds \nonumber \\
    &= -(f(x) - \bar{f})
\end{align}
where the last equality follows from the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus since the integrand is continuous.
\end{proof}

\section{Asymptotic Variance $\kappa(f)$}

If the traveler journeys for a long duration $T$, the total accumulated temperature they experience is the integral:
\begin{equation}
    I_T = \int_0^T f(X_s) \, ds
\end{equation}
By the Ergodic Theorem, $I_T/T \to \bar{f}$ almost surely as $T \to \infty$. By the Central Limit Theorem (CLT) for continuous-time processes, the scaled fluctuations converge in distribution:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{I_T - T\bar{f}}{\sqrt{T}} \implies \mathcal{N}(0, \kappa(f)) \quad \text{as } T \to \infty
\end{equation}
The parameter $\kappa(f)$ is the \textbf{asymptotic variance} of the reward function on the CTMC:
\begin{equation}
    \kappa(f) = \lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{T} \text{Var}\left( \int_0^T f(X_s) \, ds \right)
\end{equation}

Asymptotic variance is different from the stationary variance $\text{Var}_\pi(f(X)) = \mathbb{E}_\pi[(f(X) - \bar{f})^2]$. It captures the temporal correlations (or ``stickiness'') of the states. Mathematically, it integrates all autocovariances over time:
\begin{equation}
    \kappa(f) = 2 \int_0^\infty \text{Cov}_\pi(f(X_0), f(X_t)) \, dt
\end{equation}

\section{Derivation of $\kappa(f)$ via the Poisson Equation}

Let $Z_t = f(X_t) - \bar{f} = f(X_t) - \mathbb{E}_\pi[f(X)]$ be the centered reward process. Assuming the process starts in steady state ($X_0 \sim \pi$), we have:
\begin{align}
    \text{Var}\left( \int_0^T Z_s \, ds \right) &= \mathbb{E}_\pi\left[ \left( \int_0^T Z_s \, ds \right)^2 \right] \nonumber \\
    &= \int_0^T \int_0^T \mathbb{E}_\pi [ Z_u Z_v ] \, du \, dv \nonumber \\
    &= 2 \int_0^T \int_0^v \mathbb{E}_\pi [ Z_u Z_v ] \, du \, dv
\end{align}
By stationarity, $\mathbb{E}_\pi [ Z_u Z_v ] = \mathbb{E}_\pi [ Z_0 Z_{\lvert v-u \rvert} ]$. Changing variables using $\tau = v-u$:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Var}\left( \int_0^T Z_s \, ds \right) = 2 \int_0^T (T - \tau) \mathbb{E}_\pi [ Z_0 Z_\tau ] \, d\tau
\end{equation}
Dividing by $T$ and taking the limit as $T \to \infty$ gives the asymptotic variance:
\begin{align}
    \kappa(f) &= \lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{2}{T} \int_0^T (T - \tau) \mathbb{E}_\pi [ Z_0 Z_\tau ] \, d\tau \nonumber \\
    &= 2 \int_0^\infty \mathbb{E}_\pi [ Z_0 Z_\tau ] \, d\tau
\end{align}
Using the law of total expectation, we can express the integrand as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}_\pi [ Z_0 Z_\tau ] = \mathbb{E}_\pi \left[ Z_0 \mathbb{E}[ Z_\tau \mid X_0 ] \right]
\end{equation}
Substituting this back into our expression for $\kappa(f)$:
\begin{align}
    \kappa(f) &= 2 \int_0^\infty \mathbb{E}_\pi \left[ (f(X_0) - \bar{f}) \, \mathbb{E}[ f(X_\tau) - \bar{f} \mid X_0 ] \right] \, d\tau \nonumber \\
    &= 2 \mathbb{E}_\pi \left[ (f(X_0) - \bar{f}) \int_0^\infty \mathbb{E}[ f(X_\tau) - \bar{f} \mid X_0 ] \, d\tau \right]
\end{align}
Recognizing the inner integral as the value function $V(X_0)$, we obtain the elegant formula:
\begin{equation}
    \kappa(f) = 2 \mathbb{E}_\pi \left[ (f(X) - \bar{f}) V(X) \right]
\end{equation}

\section{The Elegant Contrast: DTMC vs. CTMC}

To appreciate the simplicity of continuous time, let us compare the asymptotic variance formulas side-by-side:
\begin{align}
    \kappa_{\text{DTMC}}(f) &= 2 \mathbb{E}_\pi [ (f(X) - \bar{f}) V_{\text{DTMC}}(X) ] - \mathbb{E}_\pi [ (f(X) - \bar{f})^2 ] \label{eq:dtmc_kappa} \\
    \kappa_{\text{CTMC}}(f) &= 2 \mathbb{E}_\pi [ (f(X) - \bar{f}) V(X) ] \label{eq:ctmc_kappa}
\end{align}

\subsection*{Why does the negative term disappear in CTMC?}
In discrete time, when we calculate the variance of a sum of random variables $S_n = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} Z_k$, the diagonal covariance terms $\text{Cov}(Z_k, Z_k)$ are isolated, discrete point masses. Under steady state, each contributes $\mathbb{E}_\pi[Z_0^2]$. In the double summation, these diagonal terms are counted once, while the off-diagonal terms $\text{Cov}(Z_i, Z_j)$ are counted twice. To relate the total sum to $2 \sum_{k=0}^\infty \mathbb{E}_\pi[Z_0 Z_k]$, we must subtract the single-step variance $\mathbb{E}_\pi[Z_0^2]$ to correct for the double-counting of the diagonal.

In continuous time, there is no discrete ``diagonal.'' The double integral integrates over a continuous 2D domain $[0, T] \times [0, T]$. The diagonal line $u = v$ has a Lebesgue measure of zero in $\mathbb{R}^2$. Therefore, the instantaneous variance at the points $u = v$ contributes nothing to the continuous integral! 

As a result, the continuous-time asymptotic variance is mathematically cleaner, more symmetric, and does not require any discrete correction terms.

\section{Stochastic Approximation in CTMC: Tabular Setting}

Our goal is to estimate $\kappa(f)$ recursively using a single trajectory of a CTMC. A continuous-time trajectory is naturally observed at transition times:
\begin{equation}
    (X_0, \tau_0), (X_1, \tau_1), \dots, (X_k, \tau_k)
\end{equation}
where $X_k$ is the state occupied during the $k$-th interval $[T_k, T_{k+1})$, and $\tau_k = T_{k+1} - T_k > 0$ is the continuous holding time spent in state $X_k$ before jumping to state $X_{k+1}$. Under the CTMC dynamics, $\tau_k$ is exponentially distributed with rate $q(X_k)$, so $\mathbb{E}[\tau_k \mid X_k] = 1/q(X_k)$.

\subsection{Weighted Average Reward Estimation}
The stationary expectation $\bar{f} = \mathbb{E}_\pi[f(X)]$ is the time-average reward. Since the time spent in state $X_k$ is $\tau_k$, we can construct a recursive estimator $\bar{f}_k$ as follows:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{f}_{k+1} = \bar{f}_k + c_1 \alpha_k \tau_k (f(X_k) - \bar{f}_k)
\end{equation}
Let us verify the convergence of this estimator. Under the stationary distribution of the jump chain $\pi^J$, the expected update step is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}_{\pi^J} [ \tau_k (f(X_k) - \bar{f}_k) ] = \mathbb{E}_{\pi^J} [ \mathbb{E}[\tau_k \mid X_k] (f(X_k) - \bar{f}_k) ] = \sum_{x \in S} \pi^J_x \frac{f(x) - \bar{f}_k}{q(x)}
\end{equation}
Since the stationary distribution of the continuous chain $\pi$ relates to the jump-chain stationary distribution $\pi^J$ via $\pi_x = \frac{\pi^J_x / q(x)}{\sum_y \pi^J_y / q(y)}$, we have:
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{x \in S} \pi^J_x \frac{f(x) - \bar{f}_k}{q(x)} = 0 \iff \bar{f}_k \to \frac{\sum_x \pi^J_x f(x)/q(x)}{\sum_x \pi^J_x / q(x)} = \sum_{x \in S} \pi_x f(x) = \bar{f}
\end{equation}
Thus, multiplying the update by the holding time $\tau_k$ acts as the mathematically exact importance-sampling weight to reconcile the jump-chain state distribution with the continuous-time stationary distribution $\pi$.

\subsection{Value Function and Temporal Difference Learning}
Dividing the continuous-time Poisson equation $Q V(x) = -(f(x) - \bar{f})$ by $q(x)$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    V(x) - \sum_{y \neq x} \frac{q(x, y)}{q(x)} V(y) = \frac{f(x) - \bar{f}}{q(x)}
\end{equation}
Recognizing $\sum_{y \neq x} \frac{q(x,y)}{q(x)} V(y) = \mathbb{E}[V(X_{k+1}) \mid X_k = x]$ under the jump chain, we define the \textbf{Continuous-Time Temporal Difference (TD) error}:
\begin{equation}
    \delta_k = \tau_k (f(X_k) - \bar{f}_k) + V_k(X_{k+1}) - V_k(X_k)
\end{equation}
Indeed, its conditional expectation given $X_k$ and $V_k$ is:
\begin{align}
    \mathbb{E}[\delta_k \mid X_k = x, V_k] &= \mathbb{E}[\tau_k \mid X_k = x](f(x) - \bar{f}_k) + \mathbb{E}[V_k(X_{k+1}) \mid X_k = x] - V_k(x) \nonumber \\
    &= \frac{1}{q(x)} (f(x) - \bar{f}_k) + \sum_{y \neq x} \frac{q(x,y)}{q(x)} V_k(y) - V_k(x) \nonumber \\
    &= \frac{1}{q(x)} \left[ (Q V_k)(x) + f(x) - \bar{f}_k \right]
\end{align}
When $V_k = V$ and $\bar{f}_k = \bar{f}$, this expectation is precisely zero, fulfilling the Poisson equation.

To estimate $V$, we perform TD updates with the same projection onto the subspace orthogonal to $\mathbf{1}$ used in the discrete-time paper:
\begin{equation}
    V_{k+1}(x) = \begin{cases}
        V_k(x) + \alpha_k \delta_k (1 - 1/S), & \text{for } x = X_k, \\
        V_k(x) - \alpha_k \delta_k / S, & \text{otherwise.}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Asymptotic Variance Estimation}
Using Equation~\eqref{eq:ctmc_kappa}, we expand the asymptotic variance:
\begin{equation}
    \kappa(f) = 2\mathbb{E}_\pi [ (f(X) - \bar{f}) V(X) ] = 2\mathbb{E}_\pi [ f(X) V(X) ] - 2\bar{f}\bar{V}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{V} = \mathbb{E}_\pi [V(X)]$. This is mathematically equivalent to:
\begin{equation}
    \kappa(f) = \mathbb{E}_\pi [ 2f(X)V(X) - 2f(X)\bar{V} ]
\end{equation}
To estimate $\bar{V}$ and $\kappa(f)$ online, we use holding-time weighted stochastic approximation:
\begin{align}
    \bar{V}_{k+1} &= \bar{V}_k + c_2 \alpha_k \tau_k (V_k(X_k) - \bar{V}_k) \\
    \kappa_{k+1} &= (1 - c_3 \alpha_k \tau_k) \kappa_k + c_3 \alpha_k \tau_k \left( 2f(X_k)V_k(X_k) - 2f(X_k)\bar{V}_k \right)
\end{align}

\section{Convergence Analysis and Stability}

We can express the tabular $V$ update as a linear stochastic approximation algorithm. In vector form, let $\mathbf{V}_k \in \mathbb{R}^S$ denote the value function estimate, and let $\Pi = I - \frac{1}{S} \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^T$ be the projection matrix. The update is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{V}_{k+1} = \mathbf{V}_k + \alpha_k \Pi \left( \tau_k (\mathbf{f} - \bar{f}_k \mathbf{1}) e_{X_k} + (e_{X_{k+1}} - e_{X_k}) \mathbf{V}_k(X_k) \right)
\end{equation}
The expected direction of the update at state $X_k = x$, given the current value function $V$, is:
\begin{align}
    \bar{g}(\mathbf{V}) &= \mathbb{E}_{\pi^J} [ \mathbb{E} [ \Pi \delta_k e_{X_k} \mid X_k ] ] \nonumber \\
    &= \Pi \sum_{x \in S} \pi^J_x \frac{1}{q(x)} [ (Q \mathbf{V})(x) + f(x) - \bar{f} ] e_x \nonumber \\
    &= \Pi D_{\pi^J} D_q^{-1} (Q \mathbf{V} + \mathbf{f} - \bar{f} \mathbf{1})
\end{align}
where $D_{\pi^J} = \text{diag}(\pi^J_1, \dots, \pi^J_S)$ and $D_q = \text{diag}(q(1), \dots, q(S))$.
Recall that $\pi_x = \frac{\pi^J_x / q(x)}{\sum_y \pi^J_y / q(y)}$, which implies $D_{\pi^J} D_q^{-1} \propto D_\pi = \text{diag}(\pi_1, \dots, \pi_S)$. Thus, the expected average update matrix is:
\begin{equation}
    A = \Pi D_\pi Q
\end{equation}
Since we restrict our search space to $\mathcal{E} = \{ \mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^S : \mathbf{1}^T \mathbf{V} = 0 \}$, the projection matrix acts as the identity operator on $\mathcal{E}$.
To establish the stability of our linear stochastic approximation, we need to show that the matrix $A$ is Hurwitz on the subspace $\mathcal{E}$ (i.e., all its eigenvalues restricted to $\mathcal{E}$ have strictly negative real parts).

\begin{theorem}
Let $Q$ be the infinitesimal generator of an irreducible and positive recurrent CTMC, and let $D_\pi$ be the diagonal matrix of its stationary distribution. Then the matrix $A = D_\pi Q$ is Hurwitz on the subspace $\mathcal{E} = \{ \mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^S : \mathbf{1}^T \mathbf{V} = 0 \}$. That is,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{V}^T ( D_\pi Q ) \mathbf{V} < 0 \quad \text{for all non-zero } \mathbf{V} \in \mathcal{E}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $P_t = e^{Qt}$ be the transition probability semigroup of the CTMC. For any $t > 0$, $P_t$ is a transition probability matrix of an irreducible and aperiodic DTMC with the same stationary distribution $\pi$. By Tsitsiklis and Van Roy (1997, Lemma 7), we have:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{V}^T D_\pi (I - P_t) \mathbf{V} > 0 \quad \text{for all non-zero } \mathbf{V} \in \mathcal{E}
\end{equation}
Expanding $P_t = I + Q t + o(t)$ for small $t > 0$, we get:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{V}^T D_\pi (I - I - Q t - o(t)) \mathbf{V} > 0 \implies -t \mathbf{V}^T D_\pi Q \mathbf{V} - o(t) > 0
\end{equation}
Dividing by $t > 0$ and taking the limit as $t \to 0^+$:
\begin{equation}
    -\mathbf{V}^T D_\pi Q \mathbf{V} \ge 0
\end{equation}
To show strictly greater than, suppose there exists a non-zero $\mathbf{V} \in \mathcal{E}$ such that $\mathbf{V}^T D_\pi Q \mathbf{V} = 0$. Since $-Q$ is a positive semidefinite operator with null space spanned only by $\mathbf{1}$, and $D_\pi$ is positive definite, $\mathbf{V}^T D_\pi Q \mathbf{V} = 0$ if and only if $\mathbf{V}$ is a constant vector (i.e., $\mathbf{V} \propto \mathbf{1}$). However, since $\mathbf{V} \in \mathcal{E}$, we have $\mathbf{1}^T \mathbf{V} = 0$. The only vector that is both a constant vector and orthogonal to $\mathbf{1}$ is the zero vector, which contradicts the assumption that $\mathbf{V}$ is non-zero.
Hence,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{V}^T (D_\pi Q) \mathbf{V} < 0 \quad \text{for all non-zero } \mathbf{V} \in \mathcal{E}
\end{equation}
This contractive property guarantees that the average update matrix is negative definite on the subspace $\mathcal{E}$, implying that all eigenvalues of $A$ restricted to $\mathcal{E}$ have strictly negative real parts (Hurwitz), ensuring robust, asymptotically stable convergence of the SA algorithm.
\end{proof}

\section{Linear Function Approximation Setting (LFA)}

In settings where the state space $S$ is very large, the tabular representation of $V$ is computationally intractable. We approximate the value function by projecting it onto a lower-dimensional linear subspace spanned by a set of $d$ linearly independent feature vectors:
\begin{equation}
    V(s) \approx \phi(s)^T \theta
\end{equation}
where $\phi(s) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the feature vector for state $s$, and $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the parameter vector.

\subsection{TD Updates in LFA}
The Continuous-Time Temporal Difference error under LFA is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \delta_k = \tau_k (f(X_k) - \bar{f}_k) + (\phi(X_{k+1}) - \phi(X_k))^T \theta_k
\end{equation}
We project the parameter updates onto the subspace $E$ orthogonal to the constant feature direction (if it exists) using the projection matrix $\Pi_{2,E}$:
\begin{equation}
    \theta_{k+1} = \theta_k + \alpha_k \Pi_{2,E} \phi(X_k) \delta_k
\end{equation}
The auxiliary parameters for the average value function and the asymptotic variance are updated using holding-time weights:
\begin{align}
    \tilde{V}_{k+1} &= \tilde{V}_k + c_2 \alpha_k \tau_k (\phi(X_k)^T \theta_k - \tilde{V}_k) \\
    \kappa_{k+1} &= (1 - c_3 \alpha_k \tau_k) \kappa_k + c_3 \alpha_k \tau_k \left( 2f(X_k)\phi(X_k)^T \theta_k - 2f(X_k)\tilde{V}_k \right)
\end{align}

\section{Conclusion}

We have derived a fully recursive, online, $O(1)$-computation and memory algorithm for estimating the asymptotic variance of a function defined on an irreducible and positive recurrent CTMC. Unlike its DTMC counterpart, the continuous-time estimator is mathematically cleaner and possesses no discrete point-mass variance offset terms, thanks to the continuous domain of integration. By scaling each stochastic approximation step with the state holding times, the estimator autonomously accounts for CTMC state-transition times, achieving robust convergence to the true continuous-time variance. This algorithm can be deployed as a critical component in risk-sensitive continuous-time reinforcement learning and continuous MCMC diagnostic tracking.
